Hospital Charge Analysis

A structured end-to-end data analysis project exploring U.S. hospital charge variations using SQL, Excel, and Power BI for actionable healthcare insights.

Introduction
Inpatient healthcare costs in the United States vary widely depending on hospital, location, and type of treatment. This case study focuses on analyzing publicly available hospital charge data to uncover pricing variations, identify high-cost providers, and highlight the gap between hospital charges and Medicare payments. The objective is to support cost transparency, patient decision-making, and policy-level healthcare planning.

Tools Used:
1. SQL (MySQL)– for data extraction, cleaning, and querying
2. Excel– for pre-cleaning and formatting before loading into SQL
3. Power BI– for interactive dashboard creation and visual insights

Problem
Inpatient hospital charges vary widely across hospitals and states, creating confusion for patients, insurance companies, and healthcare regulators.
Goals
1. Identify hospitals and states with unusually high or low charges.
2. Measure the gap between what hospitals charge and what Medicare pays.
3. Highlight the most common diagnoses associated with high discharge volumes and charges.

Question 1
Which hospitals charge the most for inpatient services?
Query


# Identifying High-Cost Hospitals
select provider_name, provider_city, provider_state, 
       concat('$', format(round(avg(average_covered_charges), 2), 2)) as avg_covered_charges
from inpatient_charges
group by provider_name, provider_city, provider_state
order by avg_covered_charges desc
limit 10;
Visualization
Reasoning: Helps patients and insurers choose cost-effective providers; highlights potential overpricing.

Question 2
Which states have the highest average hospital charges?
Query


# Understanding Pricing Differences Across States
select provider_state, 
       concat('$', format(round(avg(average_covered_charges), 2), 2)) as avg_state_charges
from inpatient_charges
group by provider_state
order by avg_state_charges desc;
Visualization
Reasoning: Useful for public health and state-level policy decisions on healthcare costs.

Question 3
What is the gap between hospital charges and Medicare payments?
Query


# Analyzing Profit Gaps Between Hospital
select provider_name, 
       concat('$', format(round(avg(average_covered_charges - average_medicare_payments), 2), 2)) as charge_medicare_gap
from inpatient_charges
group by provider_name
order by charge_medicare_gap desc
limit 10;
Visualization
Reasoning: Shows potential profit margins and flags pricing practices that may need regulation.

Question 4
What are the most common inpatient diagnoses by discharge volume?
Query


# Finding the Most Common Diagnoses by Volume
select drg_definition, 
       sum(total_discharges) as total_discharges
from inpatient_charges
group by drg_definition
order by total_discharges desc
limit 10;
Visualization
Reasoning: Focuses attention on high-impact areas for both clinical management and cost reduction.

Hospital Charge Analysis Dashboard

Insights
1. High-Cost Hospitals: The top 10 most expensive hospitals for inpatient services are mostly located in California and New Jersey, with average covered charges close to $100,000. This shows clear geographical pricing disparities in U.S. healthcare.
2. State-Level Price Variation: California ($67,508.62) and New Jersey ($66,125.69) have the highest state-wide average hospital charges, while Maryland has the lowest ($13,377.80). This suggests certain states may require closer regulatory review regarding healthcare costs.
3. Significant Profit Gaps: Some hospitals show extreme gaps between what they charge and what Medicare reimburses. For example, University of California Davis Medical Center and Providence Holy Cross Medical Center show charge-to-Medicare payment gaps above $97,000, suggesting potential overcharging or pricing inconsistencies.
4. Common Diagnoses: The most frequent inpatient diagnosis is related to joint replacement procedures, followed by septicemia and kidney-related conditions. These high-volume services present key focus areas for both cost control and healthcare management.

Recommendations
1. For Regulators: Focus healthcare pricing audits and transparency initiatives on high-cost states like California and New Jersey, as well as hospitals with unusually high charge-to-Medicare payment gaps.
2. For Healthcare Providers: Re-evaluate pricing strategies for the most common and high-volume diagnoses, such as joint replacements and septicemia, to ensure fair and competitive billing practices.
3. For Patients and Insurers: Use available hospital charge data when choosing service providers, especially for high-cost procedures, to reduce unnecessary financial burden and improve decision-making.

Challenges Faced and Lessons Learned
- Large file processing delays during MySQL import, solved by pre-cleaning in Excel.
- Dollar symbols in monetary fields caused SQL errors, handled by formatting the data before loading.
- No date column available, so I focused on cross-sectional analysis instead of time trends.
- Learned the importance of structuring queries around business questions, not just raw SQL output.
